About InkCoverage.app

What is it?

InkCoverage is a free, browser-based helper for print estimators, prepress operators and designers. It measures average coverage for CMYK and named spot channels in PDF artwork and helps inspect the inks and selected dimensions.

What does it do?

Analyze Ink Coverage measures the current page, all pages or a page range, with an optional selected area. Check ink limit finds rendered areas above a target total on the current page or selection. Choose the inks to count, including the option to exclude white, foil, varnish and technical channels. Read the highest included-ink total and the percentage of the checked area above the limit, then use Heatmap or Overlay to locate those areas.

Who is it for?

For estimators who need quick artwork checks and useful coverage data, and technicians who need to inspect separations or concentrated heavy ink areas. InkCoverage is a practical helper rather than a full preflight suite.

Coverage can support ink-consumption estimates, but does not directly calculate ink mass or cost and does not certify print readiness. These also depend on the ink, substrate and printing process. Confirm production limits with your printer and use suitable preflight checks.

What else can I check?

Channel checkboxes help identify where each ink is used, including foil or varnish when the PDF defines them as named spot channels. Alt-click isolates a channel; black preview makes very light inks visible. Drawing a selection gives approximate dimensions for a label or keyline. Copy, CSV and PDF Report collect all completed page measurements; reports also include completed ink-limit checks and profile information.

Why does it exist?

It was built out of a real need. Working in the printing industry, a lightweight tool was needed to quickly check ink coverage on incoming PDF files — something that didn't require launching a full prepress application just to get a number. Nothing satisfying existed for free. So it was built, and then made available to everyone who has the same problem.

How does it work?

Files are processed server-side using Ghostscript — the industry-standard open-source PostScript and PDF interpreter — with a Python backend managing the analysis pipeline. Each channel is separated and measured independently. New previews and analyses expire 10 minutes from upload; using the PDF does not extend this time. Cleanup runs on startup and every 60 seconds. Temporary outputs are removed when processing finishes, and cleanup retries any leftovers. Expired originals are normally removed on the first cleanup after expiry; active processing can finish first, and removal failures are logged and retried, so physical deletion may take longer. Closing the document only clears this browser view. Completed results remain exportable on their pages until you close or replace the document or change the selection. Upload the PDF again for new work.
