Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about ink coverage, print standards, and how InkCoverage.app works.

What is ink coverage?

Measure average coverage for each CMYK and spot-color channel across a PDF page or selected area. Each channel value is its average ink coverage across the measured area, including blank space and tints. Combined averages add the channel values: 100% cyan plus 100% magenta gives 200%. CMYK and spot totals are shown separately; the all-channel sum includes both. None of these totals is the maximum local coverage. Coverage can support ink-consumption estimates, but does not directly calculate ink mass or cost and does not certify print readiness. These also depend on the ink, substrate and printing process. Confirm production limits with your printer and use suitable preflight checks.

What is TAC (Total Area Coverage)?

Local TAC adds the ink percentages at one point. Analyze Ink Coverage reports averages across the measured area; a low average can hide a small heavy-ink area. Check ink limit examines the rendered pixels of the current page or selection and reports the highest total and the percentage of that area above your limit, using only the included channels. Confirm the target limit with your printer for the intended process and substrate.

How are spot colors displayed?

Named spot separations rendered by Ghostscript are listed separately from CMYK. Their swatches and channel previews use the color defined in the PDF, rather than a color guessed from the ink name. A channel named P485 may therefore appear green if that is its document definition. White, foil, varnish and cutting guides may also be spot channels. Use the channel checkboxes to inspect their location; the name alone does not establish their production purpose.

How accurate is the ink coverage measurement?

Results are practical estimates of average ink coverage across a page or selection. Fine details, small selections, colour conversion, overprint and transparency can affect results. Two decimal places do not guarantee accuracy; check colour-critical jobs against your printer’s separations.

Can I measure a selected area or approximate label size?

Yes. Drag on the page preview, then adjust the corner handles. The selected width, height and area are shown in the document panel. This can help estimate label or keyline dimensions, but it is a manual selection rather than automatic dieline recognition. Analyze coverage for that area or select it as the scope of an ink-limit check. Without a selection, use the whole page.

What PDF files are supported?

Current upload limits are 50 MB (52,428,800 bytes) per PDF and 100 pages per document. Processing depends on Ghostscript and the PDF content; not every PDF is guaranteed to work. Encrypted, malformed or complex files may fail or need further checking. RGB, Lab and ICC-based content can undergo color conversion, so verify color-critical results against your production workflow.

What does RGB content detected mean, and where are profiles shown?

The current page contains RGB content, which must be converted to estimate CMYK coverage. Results may differ from your printer’s separations. For more reliable results, use a PDF converted with your printer’s recommended CMYK profile. Profile information and color-inspection notices are retained in Copy, CSV and PDF Report; they are omitted from the results pane to keep it compact. Detected profile names are informational and do not select the conversion profile. Incomplete inspection is identified in the exports.

How do I find areas above an ink limit?

Open Check ink limit, set the target percentage, choose the channels to count, and check the current page or selection. White, foil, varnish and other technical channels can be excluded individually. Results give the highest included-ink total and the percentage of the checked area above the limit. The check uses a 150-DPI rendering, so very fine features may be missed. It does not modify the artwork or certify a printing condition.

What is the difference between Heatmap and Overlay?

Heatmap shows only the colored ink-limit map on a blank page. Overlay puts the same map over the document so you can locate the affected artwork. Click the active button to return to the document alone. The map and readings refer to the checked page or selection and the inks included in that check.

Can I isolate a channel, including white, foil or varnish?

After coverage analysis, use the checkboxes beside the ink swatches to show or hide channels. Alt-click a channel to show it alone; Alt-click it again to restore all channels. All channels also restores the full selection. Enable Channel preview in black below the list to make light inks visible. The PDF must define foil or varnish as a separate channel for this to locate them. Preview choices affect only the view; they do not change measurements, exports or the channels included in an ink-limit check.

Can I analyze and export several pages?

Analyze Ink Coverage offers current page, all pages and page range. If an area is selected, the same position and dimensions apply on each chosen page; pages too small for it are skipped. Copy, CSV and Report include all completed page measurements, even when another page is displayed. The PDF report includes previews, dimensions, ink averages, profiles and completed ink-limit checks. Partial batches are labeled. Each analyzed page uses the analysis allowance; ink-limit checks are run separately on the current page.

How many uploads, previews and analyses can I make?

The current allowances are 10 upload requests, 120 page-preview requests and 30 analysis requests per IP address in a rolling one-hour window. People sharing a network or VPN may share these allowances. Each page in a batch counts as a separate analysis request; accepting a 100-page PDF does not mean you can analyze all 100 pages within one hour. Requests admitted to an allowance can count even if processing later fails or the server is busy. Allowance becomes available as earlier requests leave the one-hour window. If you reach a limit, wait before retrying.

Why can a large page be rejected even when the PDF is under 50 MB?

File size alone does not determine processing work. The current limits are 20 million rendered pixels for a preview, 5 million for analysis, and 10,000 pixels on either side of the rendered page. These limits depend on page dimensions and rendering resolution, so a preview may work while analysis is rejected. Selecting a small area still requires rendering the full page and does not bypass these limits. The tool does not automatically lower resolution to make a page fit. Complex PDFs may also exceed processing resources; try a PDF with smaller pages or simpler content.

What happens if the server is busy or processing takes too long?

The server currently processes one PDF job at a time across all users, including upload inspection, previews and analyses, and accepts at most two simultaneous uploads. If processing or storage capacity is unavailable, the request returns a busy message instead of waiting in a queue. Wait briefly and retry manually. Upload transfer has a 120-second limit. A processing job has a maximum budget of 150 seconds, although individual processing steps may time out sooner. If a timeout occurs, try a simpler PDF or a faster connection for uploads. Repeated retries can use your hourly allowance.

Why is ink coverage important in flexo printing?

Coverage can support ink-consumption estimates, but does not directly calculate ink mass or cost and does not certify print readiness. These also depend on the ink, substrate and printing process. Confirm production limits with your printer and use suitable preflight checks.

Is InkCoverage a full preflight tool?

No. It provides average coverage, an ink-limit map, channel previews, selected dimensions and exports. It does not certify print readiness, correct the PDF, identify every production issue or calculate ink mass or cost. Fine details, color conversion, transparency, overprint and rendering resolution can affect the estimates. Check critical results against your production RIP and use suitable preflight checks.

Is my file kept private?

New previews and analyses expire 10 minutes from upload; using the PDF does not extend this time. Cleanup runs on startup and every 60 seconds. Temporary outputs are removed when processing finishes, and cleanup retries any leftovers. Expired originals are normally removed on the first cleanup after expiry; active processing can finish first, and removal failures are logged and retried, so physical deletion may take longer. Closing the document only clears this browser view. Completed results remain exportable on their pages until you close or replace the document or change the selection. Upload the PDF again for new work. See the Privacy Policy for full details.
